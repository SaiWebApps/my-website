AI for Non-Profits

I showed nonprofit leaders how to put AI to work for their missions, starting with the basics of models and prompts and building up to Claude skills, agents and retrieval over their own documents.

Keynoted the Philadelphia Eagles' 12th annual Eagles Care Summit on March 18, 2026, delivering "AI for Impact" to more than 300 community and business leaders.

The summit is the team’s yearly gathering of Philadelphia’s nonprofit community, and this year’s room came from more than 150 local nonprofits. Praxtera’s live two-hour session was built around AI they could use right away.

From Praxtera’s AI Basics - Prompting course, with the higher-level concepts of its AI Agents - RAG, Workflows, Skills, and More course.

How the models work

The session opened on one question for the room, “How are you using AI today?” Attendees traded what they already use it for and their time-saving shortcuts.

A model is like a student made to read most of the internet: it learns patterns without understanding them and predicts one word at a time. That is why it can miss nuance or invent facts.

Choosing the right model

Most work needs one generalist, such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot. Know when to call a specialist: Perplexity when an answer needs citations, Claude for very long reports, Copilot inside Word, Excel and Outlook, NotebookLM for your own files.

Writing better prompts

A prompt works like a brief to a very literal assistant. H4W covers who you are, what you need, why, who it is for and how it should look; COSTAR adds context, objective, style, tone, audience and response.

AI across your workflows

Then where AI fits in a working day: a long report turned into a few key points, a hard email started from a first draft instead of a blank page, and messy meeting notes turned into action items.

Claude skills

Claude skills were still very new. A skill saves the instructions for one kind of task so Claude can reuse them, the same idea as a custom GPT in ChatGPT, an agent in Copilot or a Gem in Gemini.

Skills, MCPs, plugins, workflows and agents

How the pieces differ: a skill holds the know-how for a task, an MCP server connects the model to outside tools and data, and a plugin packages these to install in one step.

A workflow runs the same steps every time; an agent works toward a goal and chooses its own steps, including which workflow to call.

Retrieval over your own documents

Retrieval-augmented generation lets the model look things up in an organization’s documents first. It finds the passages that matter, adds them to the prompt and answers from them, so its answers rest on that organization’s information.

Praxtera doesn’t just teach AI, they make it usable. More than 300 leaders walked away not just understanding AI, but actively applying it in their daily work. That’s the difference between exposure and real adoption.

Tina D’Orazio, Chief of Staff, Philadelphia Eagles

AI strategy session · PBS

I taught a regional PBS station’s team how generative AI works, what has to be settled before anyone uses it, and how to turn work that pulls staff from the mission into workflows and simple agents.

The room was one regional PBS member station: newsroom, programming, membership and grant staff, working across TV, radio and Microsoft 365. Every example in the session was rebuilt around public media work.

AI foundations

What generative AI is and isn’t, how large language models work, and where they fail.

Generative AI, in three words: advanced Google search. Search is already a prediction engine, and a large language model is a far more powerful version of that prediction, one word at a time.

A custom GPT is an app running on the model as its operating system. Retrieval-augmented generation sits under the hood: it lays the organization’s own files over the model, so answers come from that material.

Tool choice came from a programming director’s real task: ChatGPT to turn ratings, streaming and social data into one performance story, Claude to mine viewer feedback for underserved audiences, and Perplexity to benchmark reach against other public media.

Data privacy and governance first

Privacy and governance frameworks as the first step, and how they set what AI can safely be used for.

From pain points to agents

Start with the pain points that take employees’ time away from work central to the mission.

Break each pain point into a list of steps.

Run those steps with a deterministic script, workflow, or skill.

A workflow runs predefined steps the same way every time. An agent reasons, plans and decides which workflows to use toward a goal.

When the automations need coordinating, add simple agents, and let the coordination grow more complex rather than the agents themselves.

Keep each agent simple enough to audit, and put the complexity in the relationships between agents.

Examples were set in the station’s work, in Copilot: a daily rundown drafted from recent email and the calendar, one script rewritten for radio, web, social and the member newsletter, and a grant narrative built from stored impact reports.

  1. Plan Big, Run Small, Check with the GraphSep 3, 2026

Knowledge Graphs, Then Small Language Models

Knowledge Graphs & Local Language Models

Presented how graphs enrich questions and check generated answers, how to choose model size, and how to set up a local model live.

Atos is a global, AI-powered technology partner. This was an independent talk, and each graph example ran twice: once for a hospital or a bank, once for IT operations.

Presented what a knowledge graph is, how it enriches questions before they reach a model, and how it checks the model’s answer afterward,

then where graphs go next: sub-graphs by topic, failure graphs, codified expertise, and the graph behind an API.

Covered small language models next: what they are, when to go small and when to go big, a live setup of a local model in five minutes, and how they change the way people work.

Nouns, verbs, adjectives

A node is a noun, an edge is a verb and a property is an adjective. Models draft the graph, people verify it, and only verified edges are the authority.

The same shape relabels in place, from a patient, her doctor and her drugs to a client, its payment service, the server it runs on, a change and an incident.

Before the model, and after

Before the model, the graph finds the facts that matter and a verified example of a good answer. Asked if a patient with a penicillin allergy can take amoxicillin, the model gave a safe no.

For a failing payment service, the same path led through its server to a library upgrade seventeen minutes before the incident, and to a rollback.

After the model, an agent’s answer becomes a chain of claims tested against the graph. Blaming the data centre failed and went back as feedback; naming the change passed.

The graph is only an authority while someone keeps it current.

Small models fit in the room

A small model runs on one laptop or one GPU, usually under fifteen billion parameters. It keeps data in the building, but reasons less well and needs sharper prompts, evaluation and often fine-tuning.

Four sliders set the choice: how sensitive the data is, how many requests, how hard the thinking and how fast the answer. Hard thinking on secret data meant the largest open model your hardware allows.

Then the setup, in three commands: install Ollama, run Gemma 4 12B, and call the same model as an API on the laptop.

Big model plans, small models execute, graph checks

A small model does not want a conversation; it wants a task. A vague question about a payment problem got a generic answer. A narrow task with the graph’s facts named the change and the rollback.

Together: a frontier model breaks the request into narrow tasks, small models run each one on your hardware, and the graph checks every claim.
