Drone-Megaddon

A Real-Time Strategy Interface for Managing Drone Swarms

Sairam Krishnan, Daniel Ting

Carnegie Mellon course project, 2014; archived on engrXiv, 2026

10.31224/6924

I co-built an end-to-end Android, radio, and embedded-control prototype that translated real-time strategy interactions into drone commands.

The 2014 work established the command interface and execution path; Drone-Megaddon 2026 extends that foundation with a proposed language-and-vision architecture for operator-supervised coordination.

Built an end-to-end Android, Zigbee radio, and embedded-control prototype using a real-time strategy interface.

Drone-Megaddon 2026

Co-Intelligent Swarm Control with Language and Vision Models

Sairam Krishnan, Daniel Ting

engrXiv preprint, 2026; proposal, not a deployed-system evaluation

10.31224/6987

I coauthored a proposed extension of our built 2014 drone-control prototype: translate operator language into checked commands, ground references in video and track targets, then coordinate assignments across drones.

The architecture places language and vision models inside an operator-supervised system, with explicit choices about edge versus cloud execution, latency, bandwidth, and degraded connectivity.

An illustrative search-and-rescue trace shows how the layers work together; the paper reports no quantitative results from a deployed 2026 system.

Proposes language-intent, visual-grounding, and multi-drone coordination layers with operator inspection gates, audit logs, and manual-control fallback under degraded connectivity.

Illustrated through a search-and-rescue scenario.

Who Trains the Next Senior?

Managing Software Engineering When AI Makes Code Cheap

Gajendra Babu Thokala, Sairam Krishnan

SSRN working paper, 2026

10.2139/ssrn.7083078

I coauthored a framework for leading engineering organizations when AI makes code abundant but verification and judgment remain scarce.

It connects review theater, engineer development, and team incentives to practical changes: smaller reviewable units, scrutiny matched to consequence, evidence-backed gates, and outcome-based metrics.

An exploratory, correlational analysis of several thousand commits from a single organization illustrates why raw activity can overstate progress; the paper turns those observations into testable propositions about verification and apprenticeship.

Examines "review theater": approvals that no longer meaningfully verify AI-generated work.

Proposes smaller reviewable changes, scrutiny matched to consequence, deliberate apprenticeship, and measurement of verified outcomes rather than code volume.

The analysis is correlational rather than causal.

A Multi-Layer Framework for Evaluating the Return on Investment of AI Projects

Sairam Krishnan, Sonia Gandhi, Andrea Hepp

SSRN working paper, 2026; Wharton independent study

10.2139/ssrn.6732598

I coauthored a decision framework connecting AI strategy to investment and execution: assess organizational readiness, trace business value through ROIC, phase investment through evidence-based gates, and synthesize a decision scorecard.

An eight-category risk assessment makes inference economics, vendor dependency, and organizational friction explicit.

Eight illustrative public-company cases, from Salesforce and Spotify to JPMorgan and NatWest, show how the framework changes investment conditions across deployment types; this is illustrative validation, not empirical validation.

The work was a Wharton independent study sponsored by Professor Gad Allon.

Integrates readiness, ROIC value drivers, staged investment, and a decision scorecard with eight categories of AI risk, including vendor dependency and organizational friction.

Eight illustrative public-company cases examine inference costs and organizational readiness.

Wharton independent study; faculty sponsor Professor Gad Allon.
